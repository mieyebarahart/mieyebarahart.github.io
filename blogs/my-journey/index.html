My Journey into Data Analysis

Mieye Bara-Hart | Jun 18, 2026 min read

From Day One to Data: Why I Finally Enrolled in a Data Analysis Program

We all have that moment where we look at our day-to-day routine and ask ourselves, “Is this it?”

For a long time, I was navigating a path that felt more focused on completing tasks than building a future. While I enjoyed solving problems and making sense of complex information, I often found myself relying on intuition, routine, and experience rather than concrete evidence. Eventually, a shift happened. I decided to trade ambiguity for certainty, speculation for proof, and take a leap that would completely transform my professional direction: I officially started a Data Analysis program.

If you’re sitting at your desk wondering whether it’s possible to pivot into tech without a traditional computer science background, this is my story of why I took the plunge and what it feels like to stand at the beginning of a completely new journey.

The Transition

My interest in data analysis didn’t appear overnight. In many ways, it has been years in the making.

I come from a background in biochemistry and biomedical science, disciplines that are deeply rooted in observation, experimentation, and evidence-based decision-making. Throughout my academic experience, I developed strong analytical and problem-solving skills while working with scientific data, interpreting results, and identifying patterns that could lead to meaningful conclusions.

What fascinated me most wasn’t just the science itself, it was the process of uncovering insights from information. I enjoyed asking questions, investigating trends, and using data to support decisions rather than relying on assumptions. Over time, I began to realize that these interests extended far beyond the laboratory.

As industries across the world became increasingly driven by data, I started seeing opportunities to apply my analytical mindset in new ways. The more I explored the field of data analytics, the more I recognized how transferable my existing skills were. That realization ultimately gave me the confidence to pursue a new direction.

I am now enrolled in a Data Analysis diploma program, where I am learning to work with tools such as Excel, SQL, Python, Power BI, and data visualization techniques. This transition allows me to combine my scientific background with practical technical skills and apply them in a broader, more versatile way.

The Spark: Getting Hooked on the “Why”

My decision to formally pursue data analytics was fueled by something I noticed repeatedly in both academic and professional environments: too many decisions were being made based on assumptions, opinions, or what simply “felt right.”

That always bothered me.

I found myself wanting to understand the reasoning behind decisions. Why was one process more effective than another? What evidence supported a particular strategy? Was there data to validate the conclusions people were making?

As I began experimenting with spreadsheets and basic analysis tools, I discovered how powerful data could be. The first time I used a pivot table to identify a workflow bottleneck and uncover a pattern hidden within the numbers, something clicked.

I realized that data wasn’t simply a collection of figures sitting in rows and columns. It told stories. It revealed opportunities. It exposed inefficiencies. Most importantly, it provided answers.

At that moment, I no longer wanted to rely on guesswork. I wanted to learn how to systematically find the evidence and understand the story behind the numbers.

Confronting the Starting Line (and the Imposter Syndrome)

Let’s be honest: opening a data analyst curriculum for the first time can be intimidating.

SQL, Python, Tableau, Power BI, statistics, it can feel like you’re trying to learn multiple languages simultaneously while brushing up on mathematics you haven’t touched in years.

The moment I clicked “Enroll,” imposter syndrome immediately introduced itself.

I am not a software engineer. I am not a computer science prodigy. I am someone with a scientific background, a passion for problem-solving, and a desire to continuously learn.

Fortunately, I’ve learned that every large goal becomes manageable when it is broken into smaller milestones.

Right now, my focus is on:

  • Mastering the Fundamentals: Building a solid foundation through advanced Excel functions, data cleaning, spreadsheet management, and data modeling.

  • Learning to Talk to Databases: Developing SQL skills so I can query databases directly and extract meaningful information from large datasets.

  • Bringing Data to Life: Learning how to transform raw data into compelling visualizations, dashboards, and reports that help stakeholders make informed decisions.

At this stage, there are definitely moments where I find myself staring at unfamiliar interfaces and wondering where to start. But every lesson completed and every small breakthrough adds another layer of confidence.

The Mindset Shift: Becoming Data-Driven

The biggest transformation I expect isn’t actually technical.

It’s mental.

Data analysis teaches you to question assumptions. It encourages curiosity and replaces intuition with evidence. Instead of accepting statements at face value, you begin asking a different question:

What does the data say?

Even in these early stages of my journey, I can already feel that shift taking place. I’m learning to filter out distractions, focus on meaningful metrics, and approach problems with a more structured mindset.

Beyond learning tools and technical concepts, I am preparing for a role that requires translating complex findings into clear and actionable insights. The ability to tell a story with data is just as valuable as the ability to analyze it.

Looking Forward

Taking this step feels both exciting and surreal.

The path ahead won’t always be straightforward, and there will undoubtedly be challenges along the way. But this has already become one of the most rewarding professional decisions I’ve made.

For anyone standing at the beginning of their own journey, or considering making a career transition, the most important thing to remember is that every expert started as a beginner.

No one begins with all the answers.

Right now, I’m embracing the learning curve, staying curious, and trusting the process. Every new skill, every project, and every challenge is another step toward becoming the kind of professional I aspire to be.

The program has officially begun, and I can’t wait to see where the data takes me.
